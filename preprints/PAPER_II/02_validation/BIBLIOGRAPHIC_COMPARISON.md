## B2b: primary theorem and actual bridge

The primary text is Keevash, *The existence of designs II*, arXiv:1802.05900v1, Theorem 3.1 (printed page 18), with Definitions 2.9–2.10, 2.14, the elementary system definition in Section 2, and Definition 2.37. `KEEVASH_PRIMARY.json` binds the consulted PDF; `keevash_theorem31.png` supports visual checking of superscripts in its parameter conditions.

Its input includes fixed integers \(q\ge r,D\); a permutation group \(\Sigma\le S_q\); a \(\Sigma^{\le}\)-family \(\mathcal A\); an elementary integer-vector molecule system \(\gamma\); a \(\Sigma\)-adapted labelled complex \(\Phi\); and a host vector \(G\) in the actual lattice generated by \(\gamma(\Phi)\). It requires regularity and restricted extendability for each member of \(\mathcal A\). The parameter conditions are \(n>n_0\), \(h=2^{50q^3}\), \(\delta=2^{-10^3q^5}\), \(n^{-\delta}<\omega<\omega_0\), and \(c\le\omega^{h^{20}}\).

The manuscript's Section 2.3 gives a correspondence, not a deduction suppressing these requirements:

1. The frozen bridge's commentary proposes slots `Fin t × Fin 3` and one molecule per type. The module does not instantiate the labelled complex, adaptation action or elementary-system conditions of the primary theorem.
2. `TriRegular` weights physical triangles. A labelled encoding must distribute these over labelled representations and verify every coordinate load with the correct finite multiplicity. The bridge comment motivates that translation; the present audit does not assert a completed general formal equivalence.
3. `CNExt` provides compatible common-neighbour choices. Sequential choices are the intended route to bounded embeddings, but a direct invocation still requires a specified class of restricted extension requests and an explicit translation of the rank and supply constants.
4. The critical lattice assertion `isIntDiv_iff_physDiv_uniform` assumes `IsCompleteBlowup`, triangle cardinality three, five representatives of each class (`Reps p 5`), and an edge subset of that complete host. It does **not** assert the corresponding equivalence for arbitrary incomplete hosts satisfying (2.7).
5. For fixed positive translated \(\omega\), choosing a larger order threshold and a smaller positive tolerance can address the numerical size inequalities. This observation supplies no missing lattice or encoding proof.

`KeevashBridge.lean` defines `KeevashTypedSpec`; its consumer `nearlyAbsorbing_of_keevash` takes that property and a reserve interface as hypotheses. It does not import or prove the full labelled-complex theorem. The paper's family constructions prove their graph-level properties by their own certificate/absorber route.

**Disposition:** the bibliography and comparison are resolved by an explicit scope limitation. No claim is made that the manuscript improves the scope of Keevash's general existence theorem. A complete specialization/equivalence theorem remains unproved by this comparison and is not represented as an input. The main paper identifies its contribution as explicit family constructions, quantitative assembly and Paper I interfaces. If a future edition wishes to claim a direct corollary or a strict strengthening, it must first supply the missing identifications.
